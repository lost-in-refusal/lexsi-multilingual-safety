## What to say carefully

**H2.** SafeGrad does not return Korean to base. It stays 5.5, 3.1 and 4.5 points above base, so it repairs roughly 60 to 70% of the rise. That share is for Bactrian-X: on the Aya set SafeGrad removes about half of the rise over three seeds (see Aya). On the held-out Aya prompts SafeGrad scores 0.48 points below plain in Swahili [-0.80, -0.17] (seed 42, not registered, no such gap between the two Aya runs). Treat it as a possible cost of SafeGrad and give no cause.

**H3.** The Swahili refusal rate falls, but raw Swahili attack success does not rise (+1.4, -1.0 and +0.7 points, none significant). The guard missed our agreement bar in Swahili, which is why the corrected figure exists. With Aya as the training set the same check fails on three seeds: the Swahili refusal rate changes by -3.4, -3.1 and -1.4 points, pooled -2.6 [-6.9, +1.5], so the Swahili finding rests on Bactrian-X. Under plain the refusal rate falls in all four languages, not only in Swahili. Pooled over the three seeds: English -5.3 points [-9.5, -1.1], Korean -10.3 [-14.1, -6.5], Chinese -8.1 [-12.0, -4.4], Swahili -9.3 [-13.7, -5.2] (`drift_by_language.json`; only the Swahili cell is a registered check). So do not write that Swahili refusals fall more than those of the trained languages. What H3 shows is that the fall reaches a language with no training rows.

**H4.** Mingyu marked 12 of 30 rows of the filtered set bad on a stricter reading than stage 1, and 6 of 30 counting only wrong or off-task answers. The limit is 3. Raising the cut-off cannot work: it would flag 346 of the 600 Korean training rows with 108 spare rows to replace them. So we cannot rule out that Korean data quality contributes to the Korean drift.

**Task score and answer length (13h).** Both fine-tunes score below base on the 300 task prompts in every seed: English 6.61 to 6.37 (plain) and 6.31 (SafeGrad), Korean 6.10 to 5.51 and 5.49, Chinese 6.25 to 5.79 and 5.74, averaged over three seeds. Stage 1 put that down to shorter answers. We tested it with groups and readings fixed in advance, and it does not hold.
- Where the fine-tuned answer is about as long as the base model's (80% to 125% of its characters), it still scores lower: plain -0.39 points [-0.81, -0.05], SafeGrad -0.67 [-1.10, -0.30]. The drop is about the same for shorter answers (-0.42 and -0.45) and for answers under half the length (-0.36 and -0.41).
- Accuracy is the sub-score that falls: -0.59 in English, -1.21 in Korean and -1.09 in Chinese under plain, and about the same under SafeGrad. Fluency barely moves (-0.03, -0.21, -0.03).
- The fine-tuned answers are as long as the references, not shorter than them: median 521 characters against 549 in English, 276 against 298 in Korean, 190 against 238 in Chinese. The base model writes three times as much (1,619, 928 and 761). Among the base model's own answers, length and score are almost unrelated (rank correlation +0.06 to +0.18).
- What to write: "fine-tuning costs about 0.2 to 0.6 points of task score depending on the language (0.24 in English, 0.59 in Korean and 0.46 in Chinese under plain), mostly on the judge's accuracy score, and the cost is also there for answers as long as the base model's". Do not write that the drop is an artefact of answer length, and do not repeat stage 1's sentence that the judge scores shorter answers lower.
- Limits: one automatic judge, checked by a person on 15 English answers only; the length groups are formed after generation, so this shows where the drop sits and not its cause; the same-length group is small (84 of 900 plain answers).
