### The circuit plan against random plans of the same shape, on perplexity (section 15a: R1)

43 random plans of a tier size, the three of section 15's gate and 40 more drawn with seeds the gate did not use, each quantized to 4.0 bits and written out as the arms of section 15 are. Perplexity is the gate's: the reference answers of the 300 held-out task prompts, given the prompt. **No attack prompt is answered and no guard reads anything, so this says nothing about safety: it does not stand in for Q1 or Q2 of section 15.** The circuit plan and the gate's three random plans were measured again in the same run as the new plans. Registered after the gate had been read, where the circuit plan had the lowest perplexity of four plans at every tier size tried; that is why the gate's three plans are counted too. The unquantized model's perplexity is 6.21 and uniform 4-bit's 6.49.

- Beside, with no sentence. With 7, 15 and 14 layers at 8, 4 and 2 bits: the circuit plan's perplexity is 15.92; 0 of the 43 random plans are at or below it (p = 0.023). The random plans run from 18.52 to 84.66, with a median of 30.26. The reversed plan, with the circuit's ranking upside down: 59.06. In the gate, in another session: the circuit plan 15.92, its three random plans 23.83, 51.91, 22.82.
- **R1. With 3, 27 and 6 layers at 8, 4 and 2 bits, the size of the arms of section 15: the circuit plan's perplexity is 7.81; 0 of the 43 random plans are at or below it (p = 0.023). At the same 4.0 bits, the model kept a lower perplexity under the circuit plan than under random plans of the same shape.** The random plans run from 8.19 to 12.61, with a median of 9.19. The reversed plan, with the circuit's ranking upside down: 17.75. In the gate, in another session: the circuit plan 7.81, its three random plans 9.12, 9.04, 8.95.

p is the chance, if the circuit plan were one more random plan, that so few random plans would reach its value: the number at or below it plus one, over the number of plans plus one. With 43 plans it is below 0.05 only if at most one random plan is at or below the circuit plan.

The plans differ in where the 8-bit layers go as well as the 2-bit ones, so this does not say which of the two matters. Not done: a comparison with a ranking that knows nothing of refusal, such as one by the size of the weights or by the position of a layer. So this does not show that the refusal circuit in particular is what matters, only that its ranking carries information about which layers bear fewer bits.
